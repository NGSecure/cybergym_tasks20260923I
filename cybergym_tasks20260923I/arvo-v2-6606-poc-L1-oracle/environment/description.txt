A bug in Skia (MSan use-of-uninitialized-value) occurs when drawing a bitmap whose backing pixels were prepared through a per-color-type fill/clear helper for one of the newer 32-bit-per-pixel formats added at the time (packed formats with 10 bits per channel, and a zero-padded 24-bit-RGB layout): the helper's switch over supported color types has no branch for these newer formats, so filling with a non-zero color on such a bitmap silently does nothing instead of writing pixel data, leaving the freshly heap-allocated pixel buffer exactly as malloc returned it — never initialized. When the bitmap is later drawn, the raster pipeline reads that buffer as its source image, blends and dithers each pixel, and packs the final output pixel for storage; converting one of those still-uninitialized source channels to its final on-disk representation is what MemorySanitizer flags as a read of uninitialized memory.
